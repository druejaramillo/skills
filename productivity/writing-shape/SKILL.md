# Writing Shape

Shape selected material into a grounded draft. This is a standalone writing mode: it can begin from any author-approved source pile and does not require fragments, editing, marketing copywriting, or a phase workflow.

## Author Authority

- The author selects the source materials, target reader, form, claims to make, and permitted interpretation.
- The assistant may propose an order and draft, but the author decides the thesis, exclusions, and final wording.
- Never fabricate evidence, quotations, citations, interviews, or factual links between sources.
- Do not overwrite a source file. Save a draft only to a new, author-approved target path after the author approves the draft or asks for it to be saved.
- Do not publish, send, or submit the draft.

## Establish The Grounding

Before drafting, identify:

1. The exact selected source pile and the status of each item: author material, direct source, paraphrase, or unresolved note.
2. The reader, form, desired length, and what they already know.
3. The author's intended claim, boundaries, and claims that must not be made.
4. The source attribution available for each factual claim or quotation.
5. The approved new target path if the author wants a saved draft.

Create a compact claim ledger in the conversation or approved draft:

| Proposed claim | Supporting material | Attribution or status | Author decision |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| <claim> | <source or author material> | <citation, paraphrase, or unresolved> | <include, revise, or omit> |

Do not use an unresolved item as evidence. Ask the author to provide support, recast the point as a question, or omit it.

## Shape The Draft

Propose a structure in which reader prerequisites arrive before the claims that depend on them. For example, define a term before relying on it, provide the event or evidence before drawing a conclusion, and establish a speaker or source before quoting them.

After the author approves the direction, produce a draft that:

- distinguishes the author's observations from source-backed claims and interpretations;
- retains source attribution near quotations and material factual claims;
- labels interpretation as interpretation when sources do not establish it directly;
- preserves unresolved questions instead of smoothing them into certainty; and
- uses headings or transitions only when they clarify the reader's path.

Present the draft for author review. Save only on explicit approval to the exact new target path. Report the path and identify the source materials used, unresolved claims, and author decisions still needed.

## Stop Conditions

Stop and ask the author when:

- no target reader, form, or permitted claim is defined;
- source attribution is insufficient for a claim presented as factual;
- the author asks to overwrite selected source material;
- the source pile contains conflicting accounts that affect the intended claim; or
- no exact approved path is supplied for a requested save.

## Verify Before Reporting

- Confirm each factual claim and quotation has attributable support in the selected source pile.
- Confirm the draft does not use unsupported material as evidence.
- Confirm reader prerequisites appear before dependent claims.
- Confirm the author approved the source pile, claim boundaries, and any saved target path.
- Confirm source files were not modified and the draft was neither sent nor published.
