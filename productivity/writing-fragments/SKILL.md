# Writing Fragments

Capture raw material while it is still useful. This is a standalone, append-only writing mode; it does not require an outline, a draft, editing, or another skill.

## Author Authority

- The author chooses the topic, intended reader or use, approved source file, and what material is worth keeping.
- Preserve the author's wording unless they explicitly ask for a paraphrase, grouping, tag, or editorial note.
- Do not turn fragments into an outline, thesis, polished draft, or marketing copy unless the author separately asks for that work.
- Do not overwrite, move, rename, or delete the source file. Append only to the author-approved path.
- If no path is approved, present the proposed fragments in the conversation and do not write a file.

## Gather Material

Ask only what is missing:

1. What topic, reader, or future use should guide capture?
2. What exact source file may receive appended material?
3. Which text is the author's own material, and which material comes from another source?
4. For sourced material, what attribution, URL or path, author, date, and locator are available?
5. May similar fragments be grouped or tagged, or must order remain exactly as supplied?

Do not invent details, quotations, sources, or citations. Preserve uncertainty as uncertainty.

## Stop Conditions

Stop and ask the author when:

- no approved append path is available for a requested file write;
- a quotation or source-derived claim lacks enough attribution to distinguish it from the author's words;
- the author asks to replace existing source-file content; or
- attribution, permission, or confidentiality is uncertain for material they want preserved verbatim.

## Append Format

Add a dated capture block to the approved file:

```markdown
## Fragments: <topic or date>

### Author Material
- <verbatim observation, image, question, scene, or possible line>

### Source Material
- <quotation or claim>
  - Source: <author or organization, title, path or URL, date, locator>
  - Status: <direct quotation, paraphrase, or unverified note>

### Editorial Notes
- <optional author-approved grouping, connection, or question>
```

Keep author material and source material visibly separate. A paraphrase must retain its source metadata. An unverified note must remain marked as unverified rather than becoming a factual claim.

After appending, report the exact path and the sections added. Do not publish, share, or otherwise act on the file.

## Verify Before Reporting

- Confirm the source-file path was explicitly approved and existing content was not changed.
- Confirm new material was appended, not inserted as a replacement.
- Confirm quotations, paraphrases, and claims retain source attribution and status.
- Confirm author wording is preserved unless an explicit transformation was approved.
- Confirm any unresolved attribution, permission, or factual status remains visible.
